The pinned official Codex 0.153.4 source (`3d2ee51ca2d5db578f328aa75e20aa22c0197c9a`) auto-attaches initialized connections to threads. Its auto-attach path does not publish `thread/started`; ordinary child/grandchild notifications can therefore appear without role announcements. The canonical collab item carries the formal receiver but not its role. These are source observations, not a claim that the protocol documentation guarantees a particular cross-thread order.

Known scoped foreign notifications validate JSON/envelope and required scope, then remain Raw only. Their deeper item/usage/error payload is not decoded by the parent's parser. Status retains its explicit formal status validator. No foreign terminal can complete or duplicate the parent, and unrelated IDs do not gain identities or trigger lookups. Missing/malformed scope and the selected parent's wrong-turn/duplicate rules remain strict. `thread/started` retains the existing direct-parent role proof in FIFO order, before the parent terminal; queued or trailing announcements cannot bypass that boundary.

A current parent spawn receiver lacking usable metadata may obtain the second proof through `thread/read` with `includeTurns=false`. There is one worker, at most 128 distinct requested receivers per turn, no replayed requests, a one-second per-call deadline and a two-second total terminal settlement budget (the final 100 ms reserved for cancellation). The parent terminal seals admission. Results are applied by the owner, after join, before its public terminal and Result freeze. Worker callbacks never publish Events or mutate run state.

Missing or unsupported metadata is an unresolved observation, not fabricated failure or success. First invalid ID/parent/role evidence creates a sticky rejected identity and a safe notice. Contradiction of an already accepted direct-child identity fails at the merge immediately. Accepted and rejected entries share the same 128-entry table; saturation cannot let an unrecorded invalid ID heal later. The original 127/128 conflict and replay cases remain present.

Any context-ended abandoned call stops the queue and permanently pauses subsequent optional reads on that resident Process. A cancelled waiter does not prove that its wire response has arrived. A late response remains owned by the original request ID and contributes only to the Raw capture interval in which it is actually received; it cannot supply a later turn's capabilities or change an earlier Result. The real-process A-to-B fixture proves this and verifies no further reads are issued.

Synchronous writes can block even after context cancellation. If settlement fails, the process owner closes the owned transport before joining under bounded cleanup. The real blocked-pipe fixture preserves the actual write error, process Wait evidence, parent terminal/partial output/usage, no checkpoint and no reusable process. Only an error tree whose leaves are all Canceled/DeadlineExceeded is treated as pure optional cancellation. Actual write/EOF/closed and custom causes remain joined under neutral `child metadata call` wording; established caller/parent causes are preserved first.

Old tests that treated every foreign notification as fatal now prove Raw-only isolation and failure when the parent terminal is missing. Their scenarios, Raw/checkpoint assertions and capacity cases remain. Previously accepted identity conflicts still fail immediately, with Started-to-Interrupted behavior preserved. One-shot and resident real-stdio cases preserve formal parent outcomes, usage, transcript, complete Raw and public terminal lifecycle. No diagnostic instrumentation or new runtime hook is introduced.
